The mark has one non-negotiable job: survive at 16 px in a browser toolbar and still read as the same tool on the landing page, the .md footer, and the PDF. A wordmark can't do that — the glyph has to. Below, four directions at the real MV3 size ladder (128 / 48 / 32 / 16), on ink and paper, with wordmark lockups. Walk them one at a time.

The Strike

Direction A · ✶ reframed

Keeps the existing ✶ brand glyph but redraws it as an asymmetric, directional spark — struck off steel, not a symmetric decorative star. Unequal rays, an off-center hot core, one dominant up-right strike. The recoil is the gesture of Fire: ignition on the service line.

The Mono M

Direction B · type IS the brand

The mark is Departure Mono's own DNA: a pixel-grid M built from the same coarse cells the display face is drawn on. The single flame pixel sits in the M's central valley — the one heat moment. Brutally literal, ticket-printer honest, and it scales to 16px because it's already a bitmap.

DecisionLet the type system be the identity. The mark and the wordmark are literally the same face — maximum coherence, zero invented chrome.

The Spike

Direction C · the rail

The most narrative direction. In a pro kitchen, orders get speared on the rail — the ticket spike. The mark is one order slip on the spike, torn at the bottom (perforation), with a single flame line: the fired item. Pure Bear service-line iconography. Mise captures one ticket and you walk.

The Score

Direction D · the grid

The most abstract / Pentagram. A scored square — the crosshatch you cut into pork-belly skin, and the grid of mise en place: everything in its place. One cell is fired. Hairline geometry, no representation, reads as a considered system rather than an illustration.
